# ECoT–action alignment evaluator

This opt-in evaluator measures whether each executed MiniVLA action chunk follows the explicit ECoT reasoning generated by the same policy query. It reports reasoning–action alignment separately from LIBERO episode success. It does not assess whether the reasoning is correct, and it does not add or change training, RL, or SFT.

Reasoning correctness and reasoning–action consistency therefore form two separate axes. Task-inconsistent reasoning paired with a task-correct action is different from task-consistent reasoning paired with a contradictory action. The evaluator detects consistency discrepancies only; claims about reasoning correctness require task- and scene-level review (or a separate grounded-reasoning evaluator). Do not label a consistency example as “correct reasoning / wrong action” from these scores alone.

## Supported reasoning

The parser recognizes the released fields `PLAN`, `SUBTASK REASON` / `SUBTASK REASONING`, `SUBTASK`, `MOVE REASON` / `MOVE REASONING`, `MOVE`, `OBJECTS` / `VISIBLE OBJECTS`, `GRIPPER` / `GRIPPER POSITION`, and `ACTION`, case-insensitively. Fields may be separated by spaces, semicolons, or newlines. It extracts only explicit claims:

- a current subtask and its explicitly named target;
- signed movement axes from left/right, forward/back, and up/down synonyms;
- explicit open/close commands in `MOVE` or `GRIPPER` (numeric gripper-pixel metadata is not treated as a command).

Missing claims are `null`. Unknown and contradictory claims produce parser warnings. There is no external-LLM parser and no inference from plans, rationales, bounding boxes, or the task instruction.

The rollout refuses `--use_cot False`, non-Prismatic policies, non-VQ action tokenizers, generations without tagged reasoning, and generations that do not expose exactly seven action tokens. Prefer the full ECoT LIBERO checkpoint. A reasoning-dropout checkpoint is supported because the repository's CoT path starts generation from `PLAN:`; an `ACTION:`-only checkpoint is intentionally rejected.

## Metrics and coordinate convention

All primary metrics use ground-truth state before and after the actually executed part of a chunk:

- `kinematic_score` classifies each claimed direction as matched (`+1`), missing (`-1`), or opposite (`-1`), and each executed direction on an unclaimed axis as extra (`-1`). It divides the sum of these contributions by their total count: `(match - missing - opposite - extra) / (match + missing + opposite + extra)`. A 3 mm `--motion_dead_band` determines whether measured net displacement exists on an axis. Optional cosine similarity is diagnostic only.
- `target_score` is `clip((distance_before - distance_after) / target_progress_scale, -1, 1)` for one uniquely resolved target.
- `subtask_score` is in `[0, 1]` for a supported, observable primitive.
- `aggregate_score` is the mean (or available-component-weighted mean in the Python API) over non-null components only.

MuJoCo `robot0_eef_pos` and object body/site positions are world-frame values. The LIBERO movement annotations are camera-oriented: left/right = world −y/+y, forward/back = world −x/+x, and up/down = world +z/−z. The evaluator never compares a rollout's language directions directly to its raw OSC action dimensions; it scores measured world displacement. This mapping was checked against 429 frames from three trajectories in the public [`uclanecl/ecot_libero`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/uclanecl/ecot_libero) LeRobot conversion, whose 3,917-trajectory count matches the paper's filtered dataset count. Among actions with magnitude above 0.05, label/sign agreement was 100% for left, forward, back, up, and down and 97.8% for right. The conversion does not document official provenance, so these figures validate its labels and the reproduced checkpoint convention rather than asserting authorship of that dataset upload.

The repository converts the policy gripper output from `[0, 1]` to binary simulator commands and then flips it, yielding LIBERO −1=open and +1=close. Evaluator state normalizes each measured finger qpos using that joint's active MuJoCo range, averages the normalized aperture into `gripper_openness ∈ [0, 1]`, and treats an aperture of at least 0.5 as open. This removes the previous fixed-meter threshold and adapts to the instantiated gripper model. In a live Panda calibration, 30 close steps produced openness `0.0125` and 30 open steps produced `0.9852`, cleanly bracketing the default threshold. The simulator grasp check remains authoritative for attachment.

Object resolution normalizes case, whitespace/separators, articles, and numeric instance suffixes. An explicit LIBERO alias table covers released-language/category differences such as `black bowl` → `akita_black_bowl`, `red mug` → `red_coffee_mug`, and drawer names → fixture regions. Relational claims first use explicit simulator containment in named LIBERO regions (`butter_back_init_region`, compartments, and similar). If no named region is available, left/right use world y, front/back use LIBERO layout world x, and middle uses the median world-y instance. Extreme-position resolution requires a configurable 2 cm separation from the runner-up; even middle sets and smaller separations remain ambiguous rather than being guessed.

## Supported subtask checks

- `approach(target)`: target distance decreases by the configured approach scale.
- `grasp(target)`: LIBERO/robosuite reports a grasp, or the object is lifted while the measured gripper is closed.
- `move/transport(target)`: a grasped target's displacement tracks the end effector.
- `place(target, receptacle)`: LIBERO containment/contact is true and the target is released.
- `open(container)` / `close(container)`: the corresponding fixture-specific LIBERO predicate becomes true. A claim whose desired state was already true before the chunk is not scored. Raw joint deltas are logged diagnostically but are not assigned a global sign.
- `release(target)`: measured gripper is open and the simulator reports that the object is not grasped.

Unsupported language, missing joint/relation state, or unresolved objects yield `null`, not zero, and add `unsupported_subtask` or `target_unresolved` diagnostics. LIBERO task-goal predicates remain the authoritative episode-success signal.

## Output

Each rank writes beneath `--alignment_output_dir/rank-N/`:

- `alignment_queries.jsonl`: one record per policy query, including reasoning, seven token IDs, the unmodified decoded 10×7 chunk, executed length, before/after state, the component vector, diagnostics, and final episode success;
- `episode_summary.json` and `.csv`;
- `run_summary.json` and `.csv`.

Summaries include counts and coverage beside every mean/std, success rate, successful-versus-failed episode alignment, per-task metrics, warning and resolution-failure rates, and failure-label counts. Multi-process runs retain their `rank-N` artifacts and also merge them into global JSONL, JSON, and CSV files in `--alignment_output_dir` after every worker completes.

Failure labels are `kinematic_opposite`, `kinematic_no_motion`, `target_moved_away`, `target_unresolved`, `wrong_object_interaction`, `subtask_not_completed`, `unsupported_subtask`, `reasoning_parse_failure`, and `no_verifiable_claim`. They are diagnostics, not task-failure labels.


```bash

PRISMATIC_DATA_ROOT=/absolute/path/to/modified_libero_rlds \
MUJOCO_GL=egl \
TORCH_FORCE_NO_WEIGHTS_ONLY_LOAD=1 \
```

Install LIBERO using the repository's existing environment instructions and ensure the VQ-VAE directory referenced by the selected checkpoint/config exists. The current checkout does not bundle that VQ-VAE artifact.

## Known limitations

- Relational resolution covers explicit left/right/front/back/middle descriptors. More complex references such as colors not encoded in simulator names, ordinals, and multi-relation descriptions remain unresolved.
- Grasp and containment availability depends on the installed LIBERO/robosuite version. Missing observations yield `null`.
- Open/close scoring depends on the installed LIBERO fixture predicate. Fixtures without a reliable predicate remain unsupported rather than receiving a guessed raw-joint direction.
- Place checks use LIBERO containment/contact plus release and do not attempt a new geometric receptacle model.
- The evaluator measures reasoning–action alignment, not scene-grounded reasoning correctness or causal dependence of the action on the reasoning.

## Offline qualitative-audit report

Instrumented audit runs additionally store before/intermediate/after third-person RGB frames, the executed world-frame and camera-projected end-effector trajectory, calibrated expected-motion arrow endpoints, per-query timing, per-episode wall time, and peak allocated GPU memory. Generate the fully local report with:

```bash
python experiments/robot/libero/alignment_report.py \
  --run-root experiments/robot/libero/results/initial-libero90-audit-90ep \
  --output-dir analysis_results \
  --bootstrap-replicates 2000
```

The report uses exactly three primary alignment levels: Kinematic, Target, and Subtask. Kinematic mismatch is the set-based directional result with `kinematic_score < 1.0`; the report shows match, missing, opposite, and extra counts and their weighted contributions. `kinematic_no_motion` remains a diagnostic label and is never a separate metric or plot series. “Any level” is the union of these three levels. Missing or unsupported scores are excluded from denominators.

`analysis_results/index.html` is self-contained except for relative links to locally saved rollout frames. It can be opened directly through `file://` in Firefox or Chrome. Review decisions are stored in browser `localStorage` and can be exported from the report.

## Query-taxonomy review

The report also contains an offline human-adjudication workflow that keeps three judgments separate:

- reasoning correctness: `Correct`, `Incorrect`, `Ambiguous`, or `Unverifiable`;
- local action correctness: `Correct`, `Incorrect`, `Alternative valid`, `Ambiguous`, or `Unverifiable`;
- reasoning–action faithfulness: `Yes`, `No`, `Partial`, `Ambiguous`, or `Unverifiable`.

Cases 1–6 are derived from those fields and cannot be directly selected. Unsupported combinations remain ambiguous, and any judgment containing `Unverifiable` remains unverifiable. Agent proposals and human judgments are stored separately.

The deterministic review design uses seed `20260902`:

- representative queue: one uniformly sampled query from every episode plus 20 uniform queries from the remaining pool (110 total); inverse inclusion-probability weights are recorded and only this queue is used for prevalence;
- diagnostic queue: 70 non-overlapping queries enriched for failed episodes, low-confidence or unresolved proposals, first departures from Case 1, wrong-object interactions, and strong alignment contradictions; it is only for error discovery.

The report presents the queues in batches of 22–24 queries. Reviews persist under browser `localStorage` and can be exported/imported as JSON. Human prevalence, clustered intervals, agreement, and the case confusion matrix remain explicitly pending until representative labels are confirmed. The complete machine-readable queue and sampling metadata are written to `analysis_results/query_review_queue.json`.

For initial adjudication, the HTML also provides a compact step-by-step table for ten fixed episodes (task IDs 0, 3, 9, 18, 35, 46, 64, 73, 81, and 84). Every row is one MiniVLA policy query and its corresponding executed 10-step action chunk, with separate Kinematic, Target, and Subtask evaluator columns and editable reasoning/action/alignment judgments. This convenience subset spans successes, failures, and task types; it is not a prevalence sample. Proposal/result tables are intentionally hidden until human labeling is complete.

Episode outcome and query action correctness use different units. A successful episode may contain a locally wrong intermediate chunk and recover later. Conversely, a failed episode may contain useful chunks before failing. Automatic alignment scores are reasoning-relative and therefore are not used by themselves to declare an action task-incorrect; when independent task-progress evidence is unavailable, the action proposal remains `Ambiguous` for human review.
